# Make your own copy of the starter

**Card 1** · Mon 12 Oct · Tools: GitHub, Claude

## The task

Make a GitHub account, then make your own copy of the starter.

GitHub keeps your code online. A **repo** (repository) is one project's folder there, with every change ever made to it.

1. **GitHub.** Go to [github.com](https://github.com) and click **Sign up**. Use an email you'll still have after Mesa. Pick a username you'd put on a CV, because it shows in every link you share.
2. **GitHub.** Verify your email when GitHub asks.
3. **GitHub.** Open [github.com/chaaandu/forgex-starter](https://github.com/chaaandu/forgex-starter). Click **Use this template**, then **Create a new repository**. A **template** is a repo you copy to start your own.
4. **GitHub.** Under **Owner**, pick your account. Give it a working name; you can rename it later. Choose **Public**, so the team can see your work. Click **Create repository**.
5. **GitHub.** In your new repo, open `README.md` and click the pencil icon. Under the first line, add: `Built by [your name] for ForgeX 2026.` Click **Commit changes**.

Your research lives in a Google Doc, not in the repo. Its template is [`frameworks/01-research-doc.md`](../frameworks/01-research-doc.md).

## Why it matters

Every day's work from now on lands in this repo, and its link is what you send at each phase.

## When it works

- Your repo lives at `github.com/your-name/your-app`.
- Under its name, GitHub says **generated from chaaandu/forgex-starter**.
- The README says it was built by you.

## If it breaks

**There's no Use this template button.** Check you're signed in. The button is green, at the top right of the file list. On a phone, switch the browser to desktop view.

**GitHub says the repository already exists.** You've used that name before. Pick another, like `your-app-2026`.
